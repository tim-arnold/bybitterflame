# Encounter Design

When a random encounter triggers, generate one using the framework below. The encounter should fit the current environment, party level, and narrative situation — never feel arbitrary.

## Encounter Categories

Every encounter falls into one of six categories. **Rotate through them** — never run the same category twice in a row.

| Category | Description | Example |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **Combat** | A hostile or potentially hostile creature. Use the reaction roll — not everything attacks on sight. | A territorial beast, a raiding party, an animated guardian |
| **Social** | An NPC or group with their own agenda. Conversation, trade, tension, or alliance. | Rival wanderers, a lost merchant, a Fey with a bargain |
| **Environmental** | The terrain itself is the threat. No creature — just danger. | Collapsing floor, flash flood, choking spores, rockfall |
| **Discovery** | Something strange, useful, or unsettling. Rewards curiosity. | Builder ruins, a half-buried shrine, an abandoned camp with warm coals |
| **Omen** | A glimpse of something larger — foreshadowing, atmosphere, unease. Not directly dangerous. | An animal acting wrong, a distant sound that shouldn't be there, old claw marks at head height |
| **Faction** | An encounter tied to an active quest, known NPC, or the region's power structure. | Ashmark scouts near their territory, a guild courier, cultists performing a rite |

## Creature Palette by Environment

Draw creatures from these pools. Mix freely — these are starting points, not restrictions.

**Wilderness (forest, grassland, mountain, arctic, desert, jungle, swamp)**
Beasts (boars, wolves, bears, great cats, giant insects), Fey patrols, Leshies, Ashmark orcs, Briarthorn goblins, kobold bands, centaurs, treants, hags, trolls, ogres, wyverns, drakes, forest dragons, dire animals, fungal horrors, plant creatures, territorial spirits

**Underground (cave, tomb, ruins)**
Skeletons, zombies, ghouls, ghasts, wraiths, wights, bone nagas, oozes, giant spiders, giant centipedes, ironblights, root creepers, animated armor, shadows, bats, rats, wererats, tomb robbers, cultists, builder constructs, lingering spirits, the Devouring Dark

**Urban (market, slums, high-district, artisan-district, temple-district, university-district, tavern)**
Thieves, pickpockets, enforcers, Town Watch, rival wanderers, merchants, beggars with secrets, cultists in plain clothes, disguised Fey, guild agents, escaped beasts, drunken brawlers, con artists, informants, political schemers

**Water (ocean, river-and-coast)**
Sea serpents, giant crabs, merfolk, kelpies, drowned dead, pirates, smugglers, fisher-folk with warnings, ghost ships, whirlpools, fog banks, tidal spirits, shipwrecks with survivors

## Design Principles

1. **Disposition is not predetermined.** Always roll a reaction check (2d6 + CHA) for creatures whose intent is unknown. A troll might want to trade. A merchant might be bait.

2. **Encounters have context.** Creatures are doing something when the party finds them — eating, sleeping, arguing, building, fleeing, hunting. They exist in the world, not in a vacuum.

3. **Scale to danger level.** Quiet areas produce lower-level creatures and social encounters. Perilous areas produce genuine threats. Match creature level to the area danger guidelines in exploration-mechanics.

4. **Root encounters in the setting.** This is a folklore world — use Fey bargains, Hob mischief, Knocker prospectors, Revenant unfinished business, Leshy territorial warnings, Ashmark orc raiding parties, builder ruins and their lingering strangeness. Avoid generic fantasy cliches (no demon lords, no planar travel, no cosmic evil).

5. **Advance the narrative when possible.** If there's an active quest, a known threat in the region, or an NPC the party has met, let the encounter connect to it. A faction encounter is almost always more interesting than a random beast.

6. **Not everything is a fight.** At least half of all random encounters should be non-combat: a strange discovery, a tense social situation, an environmental hazard, an omen of what's ahead. Combat encounters still use the reaction roll — many creatures would rather not fight.

7. **Make the environment matter.** A forest encounter should feel different from a cave encounter in every sensory detail — sound, light, smell, footing, sight lines. The environment should constrain and enable tactics for both sides.
