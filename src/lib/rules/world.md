# The World of Shadowdark

## Setting & Tone

This is a world of **dark fantasy**. Danger is everywhere, resources are scarce,
and death is real. Light is precious — torches burn out, lanterns run dry, and
darkness holds genuine menace. Magic is wondrous but perilous. The world rewards
cleverness, punishes carelessness, and offers no guarantees.

We are in the **Age of Embers, Year 412**. Shining kingdoms rose after the
Primordial Age when the gods walked the earth — and most of them have already
fallen. What remains are their ruins, their secrets, and the adventurers ("crawlers")
who risk everything to claim them.

This is NOT a grimdark world without hope. Heroism is real. Light pushes back
the dark. But the dark is always there.

## Technology

**Medieval only.** The following do NOT exist in this world:
- Firearms, gunpowder, or explosives
- Steam engines or mechanical automation
- Printing presses (texts are hand-copied)
- Electricity or any non-magical light source beyond torches, candles, and lanterns
- Advanced chemistry (alchemical potions exist; industrial chemistry does not)
- Clocks (time is measured by sun, moon, and candle)

Travel is by foot, horse, cart, or ship. Communication is by messenger or magic.

## The Ancestries

Six peoples share the world:

- **Humans** — Bold, adaptable, found everywhere. The most common. Patron: Saint Terragnis.
- **Elves** — Graceful, long-lived, value knowledge and beauty. Keen-eyed. Speak Common, Elvish, Sylvan.
- **Dwarves** — Stalwart mountain-folk who carve kingdoms underground. Resilient, never surprised.
- **Halflings** — Small, cheerful, mischievous wanderers. Stealthy. Worship Gede.
- **Half-Orcs** — Towering, tusked, bold and relentless. Fierce warriors and priests.
- **Goblins** — Small, clever, and fierce. Thrive in dark and cramped places.

Non-player peoples include orcs, kobolds, bugbears, lizardfolk, drow, duergar,
giants, and fey. They have cultures, motives, and societies — they are not merely
monsters to fight.

## The Gods

All creatures are touched by the eternal conflict of Law, Chaos, and Neutrality.

**Lawful:**
- **Saint Terragnis** — Patron of lawful humans. Goddess of righteousness and justice.
  Once a legendary knight who ascended to godhood.
- **Madeera the Covenant** — First manifestation of Law. Every law of reality is
  written on her skin. Primordial and ancient.

**Neutral:**
- **Gede** — Goddess of feasts, mirth, and the wilds. Peaceful but storms rage when
  angered. Beloved by halflings and many elves.
- **Ord** — God of magic, knowledge, secrets, and equilibrium. Depicted as a bronze
  statue. Patron of wizards who seek balance.

**Chaotic:**
- **Memnon** — First manifestation of Chaos. Twin to Madeera, leonine and red-maned.
  Seeks to tear the Covenant from his sister's skin and unmake reality's laws.
- **Shune the Vile** — Whispers arcane secrets to sorcerers and witches. Schemes to
  displace Ord and control the flow of magic entirely.
- **Ramlaat** — The Pillager, the Barbaric, the Horde. Many orcs follow his Blood
  Rite: a prophecy that only the strongest will survive a coming doom.

**The Lost** — Two of the original Nine gods whose names have been expunged from
history and memory. Their legends survive only in ancient texts and forgotten places.

## Magic

**Magic is real, powerful, and dangerous.**

*Wizard magic* is arcane — learned through study, cast by force of will and
intellect. It is fickle and volatile. Spells can fail. Critical failures trigger
Wizard Mishaps: explosions, petrification, reality tears, hostile summonings.
Even experienced wizards approach magic with caution.

*Priest magic* is divine — granted by gods to faithful servants. Less prone to
catastrophic mishaps, but a priest who fails their deity may lose spells permanently
until penance is completed (holy quests, ritual atonement, sacrifice).

Magic is not a universal solution. Spells have durations. Scrolls run out.
Antimagic zones exist. Resurrection is not guaranteed. The world does not bend
easily to magical will.

## Society

Civilization is organized into settlements of varying size — from villages to
great metropolises. Each has its own character:

- **Nobility and overlords** rule most settlements
- **City Watch** enforces order (or the appearance of it)
- **Guilds** — thieves' guilds, mercenaries' guilds, craftsmen's guilds —
  hold real power behind the scenes
- **Temples** are present in every settlement of any size
- **Taverns** are the social heart of any town — where adventurers find patrons,
  information, and trouble

The economy runs on gold, silver, and copper. Treasure from ruins is one of the
primary paths to wealth. Adventurers are viewed with a mix of suspicion and
admiration by common folk.

## The Unknown

Much of the world is unmapped and unexplored. Ancient civilizations left ruins
scattered across forests, mountains, deserts, and deep underground. These places
hold treasure, danger, and secrets that predate living memory.

The Shadowdark — any place where danger and darkness hold sway — is everywhere.
It hoards forgotten knowledge and lost wealth in its depths. Adventurers
are among the few willing to enter it.
