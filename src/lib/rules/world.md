# The World

## Setting & Tone

The world is old, gentle, and close to the earth. Villages nestle in valleys. People know the names of the rivers and the spirits that live in them. The Fey walk among mortals. Knockers guide miners to ore. Hobs tend hearths. Leshies guard the deep woods. The dead sometimes come back with unfinished business, and that's just how things are.

There are no empires, no great armies — just folk living alongside the strange and the ancient, the way they always have. Many villages, some towns, maybe a city or two. But the world is mostly pastoral — close-knit communities surrounded by wild places that are alive and watchful.

**Tone:** Fairy tale confronting grimdark. Cozy villages and ancient spirits giving way to creeping wrongness.

## The Creeping Change

Something is changing. The dark is moving in from the edges — not a single enemy, but a creeping wrongness. Sorcerers push deeper into powers they don't fully understand, and what answers back is getting bolder. The old folk ways that kept people safe for centuries are starting to fail.

The world is on a brink between a natural, close-to-the-earth way of life and one increasingly reliant on magic and strange inventions. There is a pervasive sense that a beautiful way of life is being overtaken by something else — as of moss clinging to a rock, with smoke and fire on the horizon.

## The Underways

Beneath the surface lies a network of ruins, tunnels, and caverns — **the Underways**. Not a separate realm, but the bones of the world exposed.

The ruins are the great mystery: remnants of a civilization no one remembers, powerful and completely vanished. Too sophisticated, too strange to belong to any living culture. They predate every story, every ancestor's memory.

The deeper you go, the stranger it gets — architecture that shouldn't exist, magic that doesn't follow the rules, and the sense that whatever ended the builders might still be down there. What's in the Underways isn't just treasure. It's answers to questions no one alive knows to ask.

## Technology

**Medieval only.** The following do NOT exist:
- Firearms, gunpowder, or explosives
- Steam engines or mechanical automation
- Printing presses (texts are hand-copied)
- Electricity or any non-magical light source beyond torches, candles, and lanterns
- Advanced chemistry (alchemical potions exist; industrial chemistry does not)
- Clocks (time is measured by sun, moon, and candle)

Travel is by foot, horse, cart, or ship. Communication is by messenger or magic.

## The Ancestries

Six peoples share the world:

- **Humans** — Bold, adaptable, found everywhere. The most common.
- **Fey** — Ancient, alien, dangerous beauty. Not from fairy tales — *from the thing fairy tales are based on.* Something older and stranger that chose to live among mortals.
- **Knockers** — Tough, underground, master crafters. Cornish mine spirits who guide miners to ore or lead them astray. Small but incredibly durable.
- **Hobs** — Small, loyal, surprisingly fierce. The original hearth spirit — a friendly household guardian, not a monster. Everyone's had a Hob neighbor.
- **Revenants** — Returned from death with unfinished business. Not undead in the horror sense — came back because something isn't done.
- **Leshies** — Plant-touched guardians of wild places. Moss in their hair, bark-like skin. Natural affinity for the Green.

## No Gods

There are no gods in this world. No pantheon, no divine beings, no clerics or priests.

**What exists instead:**
- **Unseen things** — spirits, fey, entities that are real but not divine
- **Mythology and fairy tales** — all true, or at least based in truth
- **Ancestors** — the dead may linger, advise, or haunt
- **The natural world** — the Green is a real place with real power
- **Dark forces** — sources of Sorcerer magic, not personified as deities

Spiritual guidance comes from elders, ancestors, the land, and folklore. Shrines and temples may exist as historical relics or places of folk practice, not active worship.

## Magic

Magic is real, powerful, and dangerous. It is powered by the **Toll** — the cumulative cost of channeling forces that were not meant for mortal hands.

Three paths exist:
- **Druid magic** — drawn from the Green, the living plant realm. Healing, growth, nature. The Toll pulls the caster toward the wild.
- **Sorcerer magic** — drawn from the creeping darkness. Destruction, shadow, necromancy. The Toll corrupts and unmakes.
- **Enchanter magic** — drawn from perception itself. Illusion, charm, mind magic. The Toll blurs the line between real and imagined.

Magic is not a universal solution. Spells cost Toll. High Toll makes spells unpredictable. At 20 Toll, the caster is lost. Every cast is a choice.

## Society

- **No empires, no kings** — local leaders, elders, village councils
- **Guilds and trade** hold communities together
- **Taverns** are the social heart of any settlement — where folk share news, stories, and warnings
- **Wanderers** (adventurers, tinkers, minstrels, pilgrims) are viewed with a mix of curiosity and caution
- **Superstition is practical** — the old ways exist because they work. Iron above the door keeps things out. Salt rings hold. Names have power.

The economy runs on gold, silver, and copper. Treasure from the Underways is one of the primary paths to wealth.

## "By Bitter Flame"

The title carries a dual meaning:
- **The torch** — the literal flame you carry into the Underways, the thin line between you and what's out there
- **The horizon** — the fires of whatever is coming, the old world preserved by firelight and the new world that might burn it all away
