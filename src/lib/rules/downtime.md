# Downtime Activities

Between adventures, characters spend gold on class-specific downtime to earn XP. Choose a tier you can afford, roll on your class table, and see what happens.

## Downtime Tiers

| Tier | Gold Cost | XP Earned | Bonus to Roll |
|------|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| 1 | 25 gp | 1 XP | +0 |
| 2 | 75 gp | 2 XP | +1 |
| 3 | 200 gp | 3 XP | +1 |
| 4 | 500 gp | 4 XP | +2 |
| 5 | 1200 gp | 5 XP | +3 |

Roll **d10 + tier bonus** on the appropriate class table. Results range from 1 (disaster) to 14 (legendary). The highest outcomes are rare — they require high rolls at high tiers.

---

## Fighter — Carousing

Tavern brawls, drinking contests, arm-wrestling, war stories. Loud, physical, social.

| Roll | Outcome |
|------|---------|
| 1 | **Jailbird.** You wake up in a cell. Lose half the gold you spent carousing and owe a fine equal to the other half. The town guard knows your face now. |
| 2 | **Marked.** You boasted about a recent kill to the wrong crowd. A dead creature's kin — or its master — heard about it. Someone in the tavern was watching. The GM introduces a tracker or threat in the next adventure. |
| 3 | **The Wrong Toast.** You raised your cup to a figure from local legend without knowing the custom — now you're honor-bound to do a deed for the community. Lose 1d6 gold to "good faith" donations. An NPC will remind you of the promise. |
| 4 | **Bruised but Standing.** Standard night out. Some bruises, some stories. You gained XP and a headache. No other effects. |
| 5 | **Good Scrap.** You won a fighting pit, arm-wrestling match, or drinking contest. Gain 1d6 extra gold from side bets. |
| 6 | **War Story.** Your tales impressed a veteran. They share a tactical insight — advantage on your next combat initiative roll. |
| 7 | **Old Soldier's Gift.** A retired fighter is impressed by your spirit and gives you a well-worn but serviceable weapon or piece of armor (GM chooses, mundane quality). |
| 8 | **Reputation.** Word spreads about you. NPCs in this settlement recognize you favorably. Next time you need a favor here, you get it — no check required. |
| 9 | **Campfire Oath.** An old soldier shares the fire and asks for your help retrieving something personal from a nearby ruin — a family heirloom, a fallen comrade's weapon. If you agree, the GM adds a short side-quest. The soldier's gratitude is worth a future favor. |
| 10 | **Challenge Accepted.** A traveling knight or mercenary challenges you to a formal bout. If you accept and win, gain a fine weapon or 2d10 gold. The GM runs it as one-on-one combat. |
| 11 | **The Old Songs.** A bard sings a song about something in the Underways — a specific ruin, a treasure, a creature. The GM gives you a real lead on a nearby adventure. |
| 12 | **Folk Hero.** You stopped a fight, defended someone, or did something the whole tavern remembers. Famous in this settlement. Free lodging and meals whenever you return. |
| 13 | **Strange Challenger.** Something not quite human challenges you — a Hob at darts, a Fey at riddles, a Knocker at arm-wrestling. If you win, they owe you a favor. Fey bargains are always interesting. |
| 14 | **Legendary Night.** They'll tell this story for years. Gain double XP. Something permanently changes in this settlement because of what you did. |

## Rogue — Wheeling & Dealing

Fencing goods, gambling, working contacts, casing the next job. Hustle, underworld, information.

| Roll | Outcome |
|------|---------|
| 1 | **Double-Crossed.** Your fence sold you out. Lose all gold spent and a local criminal faction now considers you a liability. Watch your back. |
| 2 | **Bad Bet.** Gambling run went south. Lose an extra 1d10 gold. Someone holds your marker — they'll come collecting. |
| 3 | **Hot Goods.** You bought something stolen from someone dangerous. Worth 2d6 gold if you can fence it elsewhere, but deal with the consequences. |
| 4 | **Quiet Night.** Nothing exciting. You moved some goods, played some cards. No effects beyond XP. |
| 5 | **Good Hand.** Solid night at the tables. Gain 1d8 extra gold from gambling winnings. |
| 6 | **Useful Rumor.** You overheard something valuable — a merchant's schedule, a guard rotation, a hidden stash. The GM gives you actionable intelligence. |
| 7 | **Black Market Find.** A dealer has something unusual — a vial of poison, lockpicks, a forged document. You can buy it at half price (GM sets item and cost). |
| 8 | **New Contact.** You've built a relationship with a useful underworld figure — a fence, a forger, a smuggler. They'll do business in the future. |
| 9 | **Inside Job.** A contact tips you off to an easy score. The GM sets up a short heist opportunity for next session. |
| 10 | **The Tall Tale.** You convinced someone of something completely untrue, and it worked out brilliantly. Gain 2d6 gold and a false reputation (you choose what they believe about you). |
| 11 | **A Whisper in the Dark.** A hooded stranger offers information about the Underways — a map fragment, a warning, a name. It's real and valuable. |
| 12 | **Guild Standing.** The local thieves' guild has noticed you. Offered membership or alliance. Access to safehouses, fences, and jobs in this region. |
| 13 | **Trickster's Bargain.** A Fey or spirit approaches with a deal — a powerful item or information in exchange for a favor to be named later. The favor will come at the worst possible time, but the reward is real. |
| 14 | **The Big Score.** You stumbled into something huge — a noble's secret, a hidden vault, a smuggling route. Gain double XP. The GM introduces a major opportunity and risk. |

## Caster — Studying

Researching old texts, meditating in the wild, experimenting with reagents. Quiet, risky, knowledge-seeking.

| Roll | Outcome |
|------|---------|
| 1 | **Experiment Gone Wrong.** Something exploded, cursed you, or attracted attention. Gain 2 Toll. Something in the area is now aware of you. |
| 2 | **Forbidden Text.** You read something you shouldn't have. Useful but disturbing. Gain 1 Toll and nightmares for a week. |
| 3 | **Dead End.** Texts were incomplete, reagents impure, meditation led nowhere. No effects beyond XP. |
| 4 | **Quiet Progress.** Steady work. Deepened your understanding. No special effects beyond XP. |
| 5 | **Useful Formula.** A more efficient preparation method. Reduce the Toll of your next spell by 1 (minimum 0). One-time use. |
| 6 | **Lore Fragment.** Old knowledge — a creature's weakness, a location's history, a forgotten custom. The GM shares one useful fact. |
| 7 | **Rare Ingredient.** One dose — when used as a component, your next spell overcasts (as nat 20, but pay normal Toll). |
| 8 | **Mentor's Letter.** An older caster heard about your work. Learn one cantrip you don't already know. |
| 9 | **Breakthrough.** Genuine insight into your path's magic. Reduce current Toll by 1d4. |
| 10 | **Old Manuscript.** A text describing a spell one tier above your current highest (if available). When you reach that tier, learn it as a bonus spell. |
| 11 | **Echoes from Below.** Your studies revealed something about the Underways — a builders' glyph, a resonance pattern, a warning. The GM works it into the campaign. |
| 12 | **Collegial Respect.** A local scholar or sage is impressed. Ongoing correspondence — a source of lore between sessions. |
| 13 | **The Green Whispers / The Dark Answers / The Veil Thins.** (Path-specific.) Something from your magic's source reaches out. Reduce Toll by 3, but the GM narrates a strange condition or vision. The gift is real. The strings are unclear. |
| 14 | **Revelation.** You've touched something profound. Gain double XP. Learn one spell from your path list (current tier or lower). The insight permanently changes how your magic manifests — GM and player collaborate on a cosmetic signature. |
