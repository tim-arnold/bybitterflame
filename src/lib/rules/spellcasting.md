# Spellcasting — The Toll

## How Casting Works

Spells are cast freely — there is no retention check, no chance of losing a spell. Instead, every leveled spell costs **Toll** — a cumulative tracker that measures how much magic is wearing on the caster. The more you cast, the closer you get to losing yourself.

### Casting Flow

1. **Declare** the spell you're casting.
2. **The player rolls d20** (the Toll roll — no modifiers). The GM asks for the roll and waits.
3. **Pay the Toll cost** based on the d20 result:
   - **18-19:** Spell succeeds, **no Toll cost.** The magic flows freely.
   - **Natural 20:** Spell **overcasts** (stronger, wider, longer, or bonus effect), no Toll cost, and **restore 1 Toll point.**
   - **Natural 1:** Spell still fires, but **double Toll cost.** A Tier 3 spell (normally 3) costs 6.
   - **2-17:** Spell succeeds, pay normal Toll cost.
4. **Resolve the spell** — damage, saves, effects.
5. If the caster is at Fraying or Unraveling threshold, the GM checks for **warping** (see below).

### Toll Costs by Tier

| Tier | Toll Cost |
|------|-----------|
| Cantrips | 0 (cast at will) |
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 3 |
| 4 | 5 |
| 5 | 8 |

The jump at Tiers 4-5 makes high-level spells feel dangerous — a Tier 5 spell at 8 Toll is a *decision.*

## Toll Thresholds

Toll tracks on a scale of 0 to 20. As it accumulates, magic becomes increasingly unstable.

| Toll | Band | Effect |
|------|------|--------|
| **0-7** | **Clear** | No effects. Magic flows freely. |
| **8-13** | **Strained** | Cosmetic — the path's nature bleeds through. Vines creep up the Druid's arms. Shadows pool at the Sorcerer's feet. The Enchanter's reflection moves independently. No mechanical effect, but the GM narrates it and NPCs react. |
| **14-17** | **Fraying** | Spells become **unpredictable.** They still fire, but the GM rolls d6 — on **1-2** the spell warps: reduced effect, wrong target, unintended side effect, delayed trigger, or overcasts wildly. |
| **18-19** | **Unraveling** | Spells are **volatile.** Warp chance increases — d6, on **1-3** something goes wrong. Outcomes worsen: healing drains instead, attacks hit allies, illusions become real. |
| **20** | **Lost** | Max Toll consequence fires. The character is gone (see The Toll by Path). |

## The Toll by Path

The Toll does NOT reset on rest. Each caster path has a unique consequence for reaching 20 and a unique way to reduce it.

| Path | Toll Represents | Max-Toll Consequence |
|------|----------------|---------------------|
| **Druid** | Pull of the Green | Absorbed into the plant realm — character lost |
| **Sorcerer** | Corruption / Unraveling | Consumed by darkness — character transformed into something else |
| **Enchanter** | Delusion | Lost in their own illusions — character can't find their way back |

### Reset Mechanisms

Toll resets are **roleplaying moments**, not rest mechanics. The GM presents opportunities and the player must engage narratively. Approximately 3-5 Toll reduced per meaningful act.

**Druid — Hearthwork.** The Green pulls you toward the wild; you pull yourself back by doing something deeply mortal. Sharing a cooked meal with others. Sleeping under a roof. Mending something made by human hands. Must be *communal* or *domestic* — solitary meditation in the woods doesn't work because that's moving toward the Green, not away from it.

**Sorcerer — Acts of Making.** The darkness unmakes — it corrodes, corrupts, consumes. The antidote is creation. Craft something. Build a fire for someone who's cold. Teach a skill. Write something true. Must be genuine, not transactional ("I build a chair to reset my Toll" doesn't work — the intent matters).

**Enchanter — Honest Witness.** The Enchanter's toll is delusion — losing the boundary between real and imagined. The reset is radical honesty. Confess something true to another person. Describe exactly what you see without embellishment. Sit in silence and simply observe. Strip away the layers of perception and face the bare world.

## Spell Saves

When a spell requires a target to resist, the target rolls:

**d20 + relevant ability modifier vs Spell DC (10 + caster's INT modifier)**

The spell description states which ability the target uses (usually WIS for mind-affecting, STR for physical restraint, CON for body effects, DEX for area avoidance).

## Concentration

- **One concentration spell active at a time.** Casting a new one ends the previous.
- **Damage check:** When a concentrating caster takes damage, roll d20 vs DC 10. Fail = concentration breaks. No modifiers.
- **Duration caps:** Every concentration spell specifies one of these durations:

| Duration | Meaning | Use Case |
|----------|---------|----------|
| **Momentary** | One action or exchange | A shield that blocks one hit, a flash that blinds |
| **Brief** | ~1 minute / one encounter | Most combat buffs, barriers, summons |
| **Sustained** | ~10 minutes / one exploration scene | Scouting, stealth enhancement, environmental effects |
| **Lasting** | ~1 hour / one dungeon floor or journey | Major utility like darkvision, water breathing |

No concentration spell lasts longer than Lasting.

## Sorcerer Healing

Sorcerers *can* heal, but dark magic healing temporarily diminishes the patient. Warriors lose strength, rogues get clumsy, casters lose focus. The GM narrates this — "the wound closes but your hands tremble." The mechanical effect fades after a short rest.

## Cantrips

Cantrips cost 0 Toll and can be cast at will. They are low-powered utility — useful, never game-breaking. No Toll roll needed for cantrips.

## Against the Grain

If a Caster chose a specialization that doesn't match the GM's suggested path, they start with **1 permanent Toll point.** Their Toll can never go below 1. This is narratively rich:
- A healer who chose Sorcerer? The darkness doesn't sit right in them.
- A damage-dealer who chose Druid? The Green accepts them, but reluctantly.
- The GM should weave this tension into narration — "The power answers your call, but it resists you."
